## What happened on 2026-08-28

The day was driven entirely by two ear reports from Luke, and both were right.

### First report: Calm's 303 was practically inaudible

Not the PCF. `distortion.cpp` computed
`outGain = (level/0.7) * comp * trim * 0.115`, and at amount 0 both `comp` and
`trim` are exactly 1, so the unit was a **19 dB attenuator** applied
unconditionally. Refitted against a seventeen-point sweep of ReBirth's own
distortion, which ADDS level (-4.5 to +5.0 dB RMS, +17 to +31 dB in 2-12 kHz).
Trim 0.115 to 0.6105; mean error -14.36 dB to +0.14. Calm's 303s each returned
14.5 dB and Luke confirmed by ear.

The loss was not new. It was hidden until the routing was corrected on
2026-08-27, which put a second section through a unit already losing 21 dB on
the first.

### Luke's read of the rack, and what measuring it found

He pointed out that ReBirth presents ONE PCF, delay, distortion and compressor
that engines are routed through, not one per engine. The panel does say that.
What the panel cannot say is whether two sections routed to one unit actually
MEET inside it, and that decides the engine's shape. Two fixtures settled it:

- Two sections distorted, PCF shut on one: ReBirth removes 15.2 dB from that
  section and **0.01 dB** from the other.
- Two 303s at 65.4 and 92.5 Hz distorted together at drive 110: the excess over
  the power sum of each played alone, at fourteen intermodulation frequencies
  where neither carries energy, is **-0.1 dB**. They do not meet.

So it is one set of knobs over four independent paths. The render loop is now
one chain per section, distortion then PCF then compressor, then send, then
fader and pan. The dist and pcf buses are gone.

### The three routing switches

Every mixer strip now carries D, P and C in ReBirth's order. DIST is a
per-section flag; PCF and COMP are exclusive. The exclusivity rule lives in the
ENGINE (`setDistEnable`, `setPcfTarget`, `setCompTarget`) so a loaded song and a
click cannot disagree, with a unit test over it.

### PCF measurements that closed old gaps

A drone an octave lower (32.3 Hz) reaches parts of the range the earlier
fixtures could not:

- **Cutoff bytes 0 to 48 are measured**, and confirm the old extrapolation to
  within 0.53 dB. That law needed no change.
- **The resonance knob SATURATES at byte 112.** Bytes 112, 120 and 127 are
  bit-identical; the law had been extrapolating on to Q = 9.5. Two shipped songs
  sit at 127.
- **The corner rises with resonance**, which a TPT filter does not do on its
  own. Modelled as a quadratic anchored at zero resonance.

Static filter went from 0.49 dB mean / 2.15 worst to **0.31 / 0.52**.

## THE OPEN DEFECT, and it is the next job

**Second ear report: the drums sound muted through the PCF, high cut far too
far.** Luke suspected the filter rather than the drum synthesis and suggested
rendering with ReBirth's own samples to remove synthesis from the comparison.
Both halves were right. Full detail in `PCF_FINDINGS.md` section 9.

- Not the voices: G-Spot scores 1.50 dB mean octave error with our synth drums
  and 1.51 with ReBirth's sample set.
- It is the PCF: G-Spot's 909 alone, everything else off, our render using
  ReBirth's samples matches to **0.39 dB mean** with the filter out of the path
  and collapses to -6.5, -14.1, -21.6 dB in the top three octaves with it in.
- Filtered divided by dry, 2 kHz band: ReBirth's median is **0.00 dB**, sitting
  open almost all the time and dipping between triggers. Ours is **-11.47 dB**.

The static response is fine at 0.31 dB. **The envelope is not staying open.**

**Prime suspect: the decay law**, which is the weakest fit in the component. It
came from a threshold-crossing metric with 15 percent residuals and one clear
outlier at byte 80 (measured 197.8 ms where the trend says 380). G-Spot runs
Decay byte 67, right beside it, and pattern 32's average trigger gap at 150 BPM
is 200 ms against a fitted tau of 185 ms, so our envelope falls most of the way
between triggers where ReBirth's does not.

**Next step: re-measure the decay law properly.** Fit the whole trajectory
rather than one crossing time, on a source that resolves it, and check byte 80
specifically. Every shipped song sits mid-range on that knob and no fixture
built so far exercised it there.

**Second, smaller:** the +4.33 dB open-filter gain does not appear on G-Spot.
Round 2 measured it flat with the bypass at exactly 0.00, but switching G-Spot's
unit on changes the render by -0.07 dB and the filtered-over-dry median is 0.00.
The difference is Amt, which round 2 held at zero throughout, so that law may be
conditional on something those fixtures never varied.

## Corrections made today, so they are not re-derived

- **ReBirth's distortion is NOT transparent at amount 0.** An earlier fixture
  suggested it was; that fixture never switched the unit on. Chasing it found a
  real gap, now fixed: we never read the distortion unit's own on/off.
  `distortionOn` also defaults FALSE now, because that is what ReBirth does with
  a file that says nothing.
- **"Param N is chunk byte N" does NOT extend to the mixer track.** Its params 1
  and 2 do not move when the PCF or COMP switch is clicked; only the chunk does.
  Forcing each field in turn shows the TRACK is what routes the PCF. The
  conclusion held; the reasoning behind it did not.
- **Our shut PCF is not too open.** An earlier reading of -9.46 against
  ReBirth's -15.41 came from a two-section fixture where the unfiltered section
  held the band up. On clean single-section fixtures our cutoff matches at
  -0.06 dB mean.
- An early dump of G-Spot's PCF settings was read off track index 6, which is
  the distortion. The Freq 64 / Q 6 quoted for it was the wrong track's data.

## Decisions Luke made today
