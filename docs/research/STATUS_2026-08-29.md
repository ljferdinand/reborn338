## The structural finding: this project had no listening protocol

The `ab-parity` skill has always said a fixture sounds one device with no
effects, that the master compressor must be verified off, and that demo songs
are a whole-mix regression suite for the very end. **Every listening set ever
handed to Luke was a demo song with three effects running.** The measurement side
was rigorous and the ear side had no rules at all.

He caught it, and the fix is now in force in three places so it cannot be lost:
the `ab-parity` skill, the `component-deep-dive` skill, and `RUNBOOK.md` 6b.

**One device. One INSTANCE of it. One effect or none. Proved, not assumed.**
`tools/rbsgen/isolation_check.py` reads a song and reports what is actually in
the path, checking the arrangement rather than the save-time panel and testing
device silence by RENDERING each device soloed. Pointed at Trakka Rekka it lists
twelve violations at once.

That is not theoretical. Building the listening set for the plan itself, the
first measurement said our 303 was missing 25 to 39 dB in its top two octaves.
It is not: that export's bass 1 runs through the distortion, a delay send of 75,
the compressor and the PCF, while ours was rendered `--no-fx`.

**Every listening batch now opens with the NULL PAIR**, the same fixture with no
effects, ours against ReBirth's, so Luke hears what "the same" sounds like before
being asked whether something differs. `listen-null-2026-08-29/` is the first.

## The PCF: the dull drums were routing

Luke's 2026-08-28 report was right and the diagnosis written that night was
wrong. It read a filtered-over-dry median of 0.00 dB in every band as ReBirth's
filter sitting open; no lowpass is transparent at 11 kHz and 44 Hz at once, so it
was the filter switched OUT.

**Songs PERFORM the PCF's routing.** The mixer track's param 2 is the PCF's
target and the loader read it at tick 0 and dropped every event after. G-Spot
moves it thirty-five times. Twelve of the sixteen demo songs ride it, 8 percent
to 100 percent on-time. The compressor's target on param 1 and the distortion's
per-section switch had the same bug and are fixed too, the distortion at Luke's
explicit call.

Then four more rounds, 189 exports:

- **A retrigger RESETS the envelope**, proved by pattern 34 falling from velocity
  1.02 to 0.04 in one thirty-second against an 886 ms decay.
- **The decay knob is SIXTEEN QUANTISED STEPS**, one per `byte >> 3`, rates the
  classic 1-2-3-4-5-7 series, tau = 88.6 ms / rate. Absolute, not tempo-synced.
  Our law now matches to 0.2 percent mean over thirteen settings; the old smooth
  exponential ran 43 percent long at one end and 41 short at the other.
- **All 55 preset patterns re-measured**, positions from one decay setting and
  velocities from another because one cannot do both.
- **Two engine bugs**: the PCF's clock wrapped at the bar line so twenty of the
  55 presets restarted every bar, and ReBirth does not fire the filter on the
  first tick of a render.

G-Spot's 909 alone went from 5.3 dB mean octave error with the filter in the path
to **0.57**, against 0.47 with it out. Across all 55 patterns our cutoff tracks
ReBirth's to **0.23 of an octave**, confirmed independently at a second decay
setting.

## B4: the envelope, and two measurement bugs found on the way

The plan for the day's last piece said the envelope "falls up to 8 dB too far at
low decay". It does not. That reading came from two faults in how it was
measured, and both are now rules in the framework.

**A window that straddles the next event is not part of this one.** The B2 floor
was read from the last three columns of a folded 125 ms step through a 23.2 ms
window, so it was the NEXT NOTE'S ONSET. That is why ReBirth's "floor" looked
independent of the decay knob: an onset is. Read cleanly, ReBirth's floor at env
mod 127 runs -53, -48, -30, -20, -18 dB across the knob. `fit_accent.py` had the
same cut three columns too loose; tightened, **the shipped accent constants are
unmoved and still the minimum**, so this morning's accent fix does not rest on
it.

**A monotone measure is not a calibrated one.** Brightness tracks the filter's
corner, and how many dB an octave BUYS depends on the filter's slope. Scored
raw, B2 asked for the env-mod depth to drop from 4.45 octaves to 3.45, and
batchI, envshape and envfloor all rejected it hard while the only set that liked
it was the one running at env mod 0 that cannot see the constant. Dividing out a
per-cell gain leaves timing, which is what a decay law is, and the gain itself is
a finding: **our filter buys 0 to 25 percent more brightness per octave than
ReBirth's**, which is new evidence for defects 3 and 5.

**The fix is three constants that had been sitting in the source as a probe since
2026-08-02.** The decay knob's law is a quadratic in log tau and not a line: the
line was right at the top and 2.3 times too fast at the bottom. Timing error over
all 25 cells falls **2.267 to 0.425 dB rms** and the decay-0 cell that carried it
falls **8.54 to 1.30**. Three independent sources agree, and only one of them is
what the quadratic was fitted on: the `decayprobe` taus, B2's trajectory grid,
and a time constant fitted to ReBirth's own trajectories with no model of ours in
it at all.

Regression net over 373 cells: **-0.036 dB, better.** The sets that test envelope
shape improve most (envshape -0.232, batchG -0.179, batchI -0.116) and the ones
that regress (envfloor +0.146, envdomain +0.132, batchQ +0.035) all run at env
mod 0 and regress through the uncalibrated slope above. That is the next fit, not
this one, and bending the decay law to hide it is the trade the audit is full of.

## A third finding: the regression net was blind to the voice under test

`regress_ab.py` renders shipped defaults, and **the shipped default 303 is
Open303 while every 303 parity constant belongs to the native TPT voice.** Run
without `REBORN_BASS_ENGINE=tpt` it reports `+0.000` and zero cells moved for any
of them, which reads exactly like "structurally unaffected". A change that really
costs batchI +2.214 and 30 of its 40 cells scores +0.000 that way. Its own
baseline gives it away, batchI reads 7.646 on Open303 and 1.789 on TPT.

Some `+0.000` entries in `FINDINGS_AUDIT.md` may be this artifact rather than a
result. The rule is now in `CLAUDE.md` and in the `ab-parity` skill: put the flag
on both sides, and before believing a `+0.000`, move the constant absurdly far
and confirm the score moves at all.

## And the cutoff refit turned out to be unshippable

The day's other candidate, B1's `CUT_SCALE` 0.88 with `CUT_SLOPE` 3.24, was
"measured and waiting only on regression". The regression was run after B4, with
the engine flag: **+2.094 dB over 373 cells, 327 worse, and twelve of the sixteen
sets with zero cells better.** Each half fails alone. Scanning the anchor both
ways, every upward move is uniformly bad and the downward moves are mixed, so
**the net and B1 disagree about the SIGN**, not just the size, and the shipped
anchor sits near the net's optimum.

The obvious suspicion, that B1's wide-open denominator is a lowpass in the audio
band on our side and not on ReBirth's, was tested and does not hold: the two
`b1_ref` spectra track to within 3.5 dB at the fundamental and 2 dB above 440 Hz.

What is left is that the two measurements are not measuring the same quantity.

## Then the plane was DRAWN, and defect 3 dissolved

Every 303 measurement in this project had been two numbers per cell, and two
numbers cannot say which of two measurements is confused. matplotlib was
installed the whole time and had never been used. `plot_response.py` draws all 45
cells of B1's plane with ReBirth and ours overlaid, and the answer is visible in
about ten seconds: **the corner positions lie almost on top of each other**, and
what differs is the balance either side of the corner.

**B1's corner statistic reads "3 dB below its own PASSBAND", and takes the
passband from the lowest four partials of a 55 Hz drone.** Wherever the corner
comes down near 65 to 231 Hz that reading is already in the stopband, which is
most of the low-cutoff half of the plane. The denominator rule again, in a new
disguise: a reference taken from inside the thing being measured.

Two passband-free measures, the resonant peak frequency and the crossing of an
absolute -20 dB, agree with each other and put **our corner within 0.08 octaves**.
Defect 3 does not exist. The refit was fixing something that was not broken,
which is exactly why the net rejected it and disagreed about the sign.

**What is actually wrong is a SEE-SAW about the corner:** at every resonance from
64 up we put too much energy in the three octaves below it and too little at and
above it. **The size first published, 5 dB and 5 to 10 dB, is not confirmed and
is probably inflated**, because it too was computed over `b1_ref`. Cut a way that
needs no reference at all, each cell over the same cutoff at resonance 0, the
effect is about a third of that: our peak comes up 1.5 to 3.5 dB short and our
skirts run 1 to 3 dB full. Direction stands, size does not, and batch B5 exists
to settle it. That one shape explains all three disagreements.
The net scores energy-normalised shape, so our excess low end dominates and
moving the corner up makes it worse. B2's brightness gain is the same see-saw
through a band ratio. And "the peak is 11.5 dB short" was measured over the
passband: split apart, the absolute peak is within a few dB and errs BOTH ways
across the knob, up to 9.2 dB tall at low cutoff and 6.5 short at high, while the
passband runs 2 to 8.6 dB loud almost everywhere.

## The level law, fitted and shipped (batch B6)

`LVL_CUT` 8.4 dB of tilt across the cutoff knob at resonance 0, gated away by
resonance with a constant of 0.10 and pivoting at 0.65 of the knob. A gain,
applied with the passband makeup.

**It is a gain and not a corner, and that took two wrong attempts.** Widening the
cutoff knob to `CUT_SLOPE` 4.25 does fix the level surface, and it drags the
resonance-96 corner 0.79 octaves low. Adding a resonance-to-corner coupling to
put the corner back still leaves batch B2's envelope trajectory at 2.80 dB rms
against 0.43 and its onset 11.35 dB dark. Three independent measurements say the
corner is right; only the level was wrong.

**A normalisation mistake of my own inflated it first.** `fit_level.py` originally
anchored each side's surface on its own `b1_c000_r000` cell, which is the single
most anomalous cell in the set, and read 6.47 dB rms. Anchored on the plane's
MEAN, which no one cell can move, the real disagreement is 1.84.

**The check that matters is batch H**, a different set measured a different way,
through `level_ab.py`, which exists to ask exactly this question. Cutoff tilt
before: -1.38 dB peak, -1.59 rms. After: **-0.03 and -0.25**, spread 0.47 down to
0.11. The tilt is gone.

What remains is a FLAT offset of about -3.1 dB peak, -3.6 rms, and that is the
condition `level_ab.py` was written to detect: the remaining deficit is now a
one-number calibration rather than filter error, which `COMPONENT_MATRIX.md` has
said for weeks it must become before a scalar may be applied. **Taken on Luke's sign-off the same day**, at `LVL_TRIM` 1.46. Batch H's level goes from -3.00 dB peak under ReBirth to **+0.11**, and the full net over 373 cells goes 2.075 to 1.950. Every regressing set is an accent set, which is the open accent defect being exposed rather than caused, and Luke's instruction was to fix that rather than back this out.

**With the rack genuinely off on both sides, our 303 matches ReBirth to about a
dB per band on real music, on both voices.** With the distortion on, ours is
19.9 dB short at 2.8 kHz and 47.9 short at 5.7 kHz on bass 2, and has the wrong

ours subtracts 5.2.

So supertask 7 is where the audible gap lives, and the 303 is in far better shape
than yesterday's numbers suggested. `listen-calm-dist-2026-08-30/`.

## Corrections made today, so they are not re-derived

- **The 2026-08-28 PCF diagnosis is retired**, kept as `PCF_FINDINGS` 9a.
- **Calm does not run resonance 127** in the window measured. Its chunk says so;
  its track starts at cutoff 0, resonance 0 and does not reach the chunk's values
  until bar 156. **A chunk is the save-time panel; the track is what plays.** This
  cost time twice in one day on two components and is now a gotcha everywhere.
- **"Param N is chunk byte N does not extend to the mixer track" was too strong.**
  Params 1 and 2 really are the compressor's and the PCF's targets; they do not
  move when you CLICK the switch because a click writes the chunk and only
  recording writes the track.
- **Three of my own measurement designs failed the same way**, a denominator that
  was not one. A PCF reference with the unit on at zero depth, a note-0 reference
  serving note-7 fixtures, and a four-pole model fitted to a ratio of two
  four-poles. The rule is now in the skill: a denominator differs from its
  numerator in exactly one thing, and a filter you cannot switch off is not one.
